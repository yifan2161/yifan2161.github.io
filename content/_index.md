I am currently a **Master in Public Policy (MPP)** student at **Harvard Kennedy School**, concentrating in **Technology Policy**. My research interests lie at the intersection of sociology, economics, and public policy. Broadly, I am interested in how social institutions, organizational practices, and emerging technologies shape human behavior, social inequality, and economic opportunity.

One of my primary research interests is **gender inequality in the workplace**, particularly how workplace norms, organizational practices, and government policies influence hiring, promotion, workplace culture, and women's career opportunities. I am also interested in the intersection of **gender and artificial intelligence**, including algorithmic bias, technology-facilitated gender-based harm, and the broader social and regulatory implications of emerging technologies.

Methodologically, I am particularly interested in **survey experiments**, **semi-structured interviews**, and **mixed-methods research** that combines quantitative and qualitative approaches to understand social behavior, individual experiences, and institutional dynamics. My undergraduate honors thesis employed a randomized factorial survey experiment to examine how employee gender and parental leave duration influence workplace evaluations in the United States. My broader research experience has also spanned international political economy, community well-being, and environmental governance.

Before Harvard, I graduated from **Vanderbilt University** with a B.S. in Economics and Sociology and minors in Data Science and Business, earning Highest Honors in Sociology. I have also gained research experience through several academic research labs and applied analytical experience at TikTok and NielsenIQ. These experiences have strengthened my interest in interdisciplinary research that connects empirical evidence with real-world policy challenges.
